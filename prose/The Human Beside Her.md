# The Human Beside Her

## Prologue

The mountain trail was new and the blood was fresh.

Too dark for human. Too much of it for anything still walking. He crouched at the edge of the stain and pressed two fingers into the dirt, rubbing the residue between them out of habit, though it told him nothing he didn't already know. Demon. Big one. Moving slow, moving hurt, moving up.

He wiped his hand on his cloak and checked his sword.

The Guild had sent him on another cleanup job — scattered soldiers, remnants of the invasion force that had broken against the allied defense three months ago. Most of what was left out here were stragglers. Lost, hungry things that wandered too close to a village and needed to be put down before a shepherd or a child paid the price. Magnus called it cleanup. Simple work. Important, but simple.

The report on this one had been different. A traveler, half out of his mind with fear, had stumbled into Thornwall babbling about something in the high passes. Something that moved wrong. Something that made the air feel heavy just by being near it. The other hunters had laughed it off — altitude sickness, a trick of the light, a man who'd had too much to drink before a cold hike.

Magnus hadn't laughed. He'd made the assignment and said three words: "Trust your gut."

So the demon hunter climbed.

The blood trail wound through a narrow cut between two rock faces, the kind of passage that funneled sound and swallowed light. He kept his sword low and his breathing quiet. Three months of hunting demons had taught him what the Guild's training manuals hadn't — that the dangerous ones were never the ones that roared. The dangerous ones were the ones that chose where you found them.

He rounded the last outcrop with his weapon ready, every muscle coiled for the thing that was supposed to happen next. The lunge. The snarl. The collision of steel and claw that his body knew like a heartbeat.

It didn't come.

She was propped against the rock face, one arm pressed tight against her side, dark blood seeping between her fingers. The horns were unmistakable — not the crude, crooked things he'd seen on foot soldiers, but elegant, swept-back curves that caught the fading light like a crown.

He knew her face. Every hunter did. It was on every bounty board between here and the coast, printed beneath a number with too many zeroes to take seriously.

The demon queen looked up at him with eyes that held no fear, no fury, no plea for mercy. Just exhaustion so deep it had worn through everything else and come out the other side as something that looked like patience.

She watched him calculate. Watched his grip shift on the sword. Watched the distance between them shrink to the length of a single desperate lunge, and she did not move.

When she spoke, her voice was rough and steady.

"Wait. Before you do what you came to do — I know what I'm worth. Probably more than you'd make in ten lifetimes. I'm not going to insult you by begging."
